# Foundation #18 — original-world diplomacy continuity and source evidence

The original Unified Master Prompt §§33–36 requires politically independent countries with distinct alliances, commerce, conflict, leaders and living consequences. **The actual** `world/simulation.js::tickMonth` already simulates the relationships in `w.diplomacy` and their downstream economic, route, war and stability consequences. It makes genuine independently resolved **model-level geopolitical status transitions**: cooperation, strained relations, peace, armed conflict and truce. A state relationship being marked as cooperative does **not** prove a particular minister signed a public treaty; a cessation of simulated fighting does **not** prove named non-player diplomats consented or negotiated certain terms.

This in-place bridge **does not introduce a new diplomacy engine or additional random draw**. The original diplomatic relation itself now retains an `diplomaticAudit` field with its authentic initial/old-save status, and every subsequently *actual*, independent world-month status transition that its existing algorithm genuinely made. Each enduring source captures the same original pair and origin, **the original world RNG state before and after its already existing one noise draw**, exact bounded tension noise, original before/after tension, economy/context inputs (prosperity, rivalry, month and original war exhaustion), original cooldown and fighting duration changes, and the actual status before/after. Source verification independently replays the original decision rules and checks an unbroken exact status-transition chain. The world-owned `w.diplomacy[key]` still controls routes and politics; historic evidence stays in that very same record after the short international courier bulletin expires.

For old game checkpoints, where only the current relationship was saved and earlier diplomatic status history was never kept, the verifier starts an explicit **current, source-known audit anchor** from the saved pair and saved status. It does not retroactively invent wars, diplomats, agreements, treaties or earlier dates. A read-only internal `diplomaticTransitionHistory(w,key)` exposes the provenance to the GM, **not** unearned world-wide knowledge to the player. The GM must continue respecting original public-notice time, local knowledge and confidential diplomacy. Nothing in the patch rewrites a campaign's known facts or adds coins, titles, inhabitants, new agreements or powerful new foreign policy controls.

The disposable `tests/original-diplomacy-transition-history.test.js` uses a **real existing atlas rival pair** and two genuine original world-month advances in a disposable game, staging extreme pressure to exercise the actual original war and truce decisions. It checks original war/peace counters, continued player RNG and money isolation, original source-random-state proof, source-sealed consecutive status transitions, save export/import, tamper rejection, and a truthful old-checkpoint anchor without invented history. The complete inventory becomes **180 test groups** on the stacked PR branch, prior to any merge acceptance.

**Knowledge and verification boundary:** Only recorded future *status changes* append a transition source. No status change in a given month does not establish that no internal contacts, discussions or ordinary trade occurred; those must come from separate genuine evidence. Likewise a saved relationship from before auditing may have substantial unrecorded history, which this feature explicitly declines to reconstruct. Existing public courier delays and the GM's player-known-information rules still govern disclosure.
