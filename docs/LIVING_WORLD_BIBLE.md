# Beastbound Living Realms — world bible v0.8

> **Canon provenance:** The *Unified Master Prompt* requires continental scale, internally coherent cultures, economic and political causality, large biogeographic taxonomy, independent agents and multiple non-equivalent power systems. It does **not** supply all national names or a fixed political map. The places below are **newly authored worldbuilding** satisfying those requirements, not facts copied from Jory's campaign. The original named places from Aerin's v0.3 save remain authoritative: Morrowfen Crossing, Whisperfen, Copperwood, Ashwind, Lumenfall Annex and Riftglass Outpost. In a conflict, the player's established scene and Master Prompt prevail.

## Geography and world history

Six distinct continental environments share oceans, trade routes and wildlife corridors: **Valedryn** (western river deltas, Crownspine mountains and the Mirror Gulf), **Sahriel** (monsoon ports and desert aquifers), **Threnor** (fjords, migrating ice roads and aurora taiga), **Kareth** (volcanic cities, sky terraces and fault-zone rifts), **Islarin** (reef-island nations and cyclone shipping lanes), and **Oruvan** (interior steppe and elevated cultivation settlements). Seas and mountain chains are genuine costs to movement, trade, species dispersal and military logistics.

Five broad periods provide causality without imposing a definitive theological origin: the **Age of First Paths** before political borders; the **Estuary Wars**, whose canal fighting produced the Seven Estuary Treaty; the **Ash Years** that dissolved Kareth's volcanic empire and made rift licensing contentious; the **Longwinter Covenant**, which recognised north-coast migratory refuges; and a modern century of transnational institutes, trade charters, beast personhood disputes and scientific rivalry. Different societies teach competing accounts; public history is not proof of every ancient claim. Cosmological truth, what happens after death and the nature of gods require more source-grounded adjudication, rather than arbitrary new magic.

## Aerin's locality: Morrowfen Basin

The **Reedmarch Compact** is a waterway council jurisdiction that includes Morrowfen Crossing, Whisperfen Wetlands, Copperwood Verge, Ashwind Foothills, Lumenfall Annex and Riftglass Outpost. This is newly authored *political geography* around places already in Aerin's save. Reedmarch riverfolk speak March Cant and use shared tide boards to organise flood work; their reed-lattice construction, marsh greens and low-noise beast harnesses follow the wetland economy. The **March Council** handles levees and river rights. The **Reed Clan Council** defends seasonal fishing grounds, the **Naturalists Guild** studies bank stability and certifies field reports, and **River Wardens** try to hold migration corridors open. Their goals overlap but conflict: a levee may save homes while destroying nesting beds. Sanctuary staff and guild registrars therefore disagree for practical reasons, not to deliver a quest to Aerin.

Elira Moss remains the established guild registrar, not a newly generated ruler. The damaged reed-bank notice and distant lanternfen fox from the active save remain in play. The date is **Day 1, 08:00**, with cool mist; no absolute year has been established for Aerin, and the world does not import Jory's 3 Migration 1147 or Jory's politics.

## Continental state systems

Valedryn contains the **Reedmarch Compact**, **Asterwyn Crown**, **Crownspine Holds** and **Mirror Gulf League**. Orchard provisioners exchange food for mineral wards through dangerous mountain passes. Asterwyn's hearth pledges can require nobles to fund common roads; Crownspine rescue apprentices must demonstrate practical competence; the Mirror Gulf's multilingual port courts recognise nonhuman testimony differently from inland jurisdictions.

Sahriel holds the **Sahri Well Covenant**, **Red Dune Sultanate**, **Copper Stair Charter** and **Pearl Bight Republic**. Public aquifers and rain gardens support cities where water is more important than inherited land. Well-keepers and caravan elders hold conflicting authority over emergency water requests. Pollinator sanctuaries face plantation pressure.

Threnor includes the **Whitewake Jarldoms**, **Icehollow Monastery Lands**, **Winterwood Commons** and **Frostmarch Protectorate**. Refuge obligations, nonhuman marine treaties and winter timber leases are material legal and religious commitments. Longnight migrations are not simply monster encounters.

Kareth includes the **Free Forges**, **Sky Terrace Union**, **Ashglass March** and **Sunforge Principality**. Magitechnic glass and basalt supply the continent, but toxic ash, cliff-nesting rights and restricted rift ecology limit unregulated industry. Forge unions can shut down production; containment wardens can close trade roads.

Islarin includes the **Tide Assembly**, **Reef Cantons**, **Stormpeak Freeholds** and **Blackcurrent Harbours**. Reef Cantons are an autonomous beast polity; their representatives speak through translators and aquatic signalling, not forced contracts. Storm calendars, protected reef corridors, island assemblies and merchant law create genuine international disputes.

Oruvan includes the **Grassroad Confederacy**, **Skyvillage League**, **Iron Citadel Dominion** and **Old Tracks Beast Commonwealth**. Grazing seasons define borders for mobile peoples; the Old Tracks Commonwealth is a nonhuman civic nation where informed consent and communal habitat rights are governing law. The Iron Citadel's military extraction creates pressure on these passage rights, while Skyvillage independent cultivators offer a non-partnership path.

The fully keyed national, culture, institution, geography and trade records live in **`world/atlas.js`**, not only in prose. Its 24 polities have individual governments, rivals, trade resources, rights codes, cultures, strengths and economic vulnerabilities. The 48 institutions have distinct specialties, recruitment resources, budgets and rivals. A culture is expressed in architecture, work, ceremonies, food and law; it must **not** become a generic modifier to a character's worth or ability.

## Economy, law and public life

The simulation tracks national grain, feed, materials, medicine and Vael indices, real trade-route volume and disruptions, institutional funds and capacity, government legitimacy and stability, and relations between neighbours. Talons/Fangs/Scales remain the accepted currency standard where already established. A numerical index is a GM model, **not a price quote**; the player needs an in-world market or report to learn current prices. Water access, dangerous wildlife permits, sanctuary rights, beast personhood, lineages, training licences, military recruitment and rift research vary by jurisdiction. Protected persons and sapient beasts can refuse contracts regardless of an apparent numeric willingness value.

Institutions and ordinary livelihoods continue off-screen. Academy and guild standing differs from human cultivation power: a renowned medic need not be a powerful fighter. Credentials, fees, exam cycles, guild accreditation, rescues, court decisions, labour actions and new research produce hooks only when causally grounded in records. Rival schools may cooperate on habitat or refuse to recognise a paper.

## The living bestiary

The Master Prompt requires **species capacity at the scale of hundreds of thousands**, not a gigantic list in the player UI. `world/bestiary.js` currently has 26 coherent ecological lineages; each has four compatible biome radiations, 36 systematic genera, 64 distinct species positions, and three regional forms. That is **239,616 generated species positions and 718,848 possible forms**, created on demand from a stable ID. The count is generative capacity—not a claim that anyone hand-authored hundreds of thousands of distinct biologically complete organisms. A generated taxon records ancestry, body plan, dietary niche, reproductive lifecycle, affinity mechanism, habitat and legal considerations. It cannot inherit invented unique abilities or evolution powers. Real discoveries are logged as local, source-attributed observations. Established named species in `engine.js` remain unchanged and take priority. Evolution branch information stays undisclosed until the character actually learns it.

The ecology engine does **not** instantiate 718,848 beasts each turn. It tracks a few representative lineage populations per region, plus habitat quality, forage, migration and human extraction. Individuals met by a character are persistent creature records when made relevant; species-wide variation does not erase individual temperament or relationships. Drought, pollution, forest loss, nesting cycles, pressure from food demand and trade disruptions affect migration and scarcity. The absence of a species from a single survey does not mean worldwide extinction.

## Calendar and living simulation

A **365-day** year has twelve 30-day months (Deepfrost through Hearthmonth) followed by five Concord Days. Normal weeks are six days, with Hearthday as the sixth; the festival interlude is outside sixday labour cycles. On a save without a verified absolute date, the engine uses the **relative Day N** counter and simulates sixday/month boundaries from that relative cycle; it does not retroactively assert a year, season or holiday for Aerin. Once a dated source establishes a date, a one-time calendar anchor can be checkpointed.

World simulation runs with an **independent seeded random stream**: sixday intervals update country resources and institutions and regional habitat indicators; monthly ticks change trade routes, market indices, diplomacy, ruling mandates, ecological migrations and exceptional public events. Exhaustion can end wars; states and academies can decline and recover; conservation, rescue, legal rights and short-term repairs matter. The engine persists state in the existing campaign JSON under `table.realm`; it does not change player dice, invent a personal prophecy or narrate secret politics as news. Newspapers, letters, field notices and couriers have location and delivery times. A hidden development is not player knowledge until there is a plausible channel. Every event is timestamped and the historical log is compacted rather than simulated infinitely in microscopic detail.

## What remains for future work

This is a **working regional simulation foundation**, not the entire finished world of the Master Prompt. Major future layers include individual named rulers with succession genealogies, legal court procedures, war campaigns with tactical armies, full profession licensing and school syllabi, diplomatic treaty drafting, travel and weather routing across a detailed map, birth/death records, city finances, language acquisition, permanent species-level conservation data, lineage genetics, dedicated planar cosmology, religious history, and robust full-spectrum human cultivation. These should be added when required by actual campaigns, using source rules and tests rather than invented numerical outcomes. The current local v0.3 prototype still contains simplified skills and combat outside the protected Project-chat runner.

### Independent advancement of people

`world/notables.js` keeps **48 initially sealed background professionals**, two per state, on their own skill and discipline tracks. Their monthly study uses the reference-backed training-rate and skill-PP maths, depends on institutional capacity and peacetime conditions, and can reach a documented *readiness for trial*. Important discipline changes **do not happen automatically**: a Meridian foundation, Arcane Circle, Soul advancement or high specialist rank still needs its own verified breakthrough and prerequisites. This is deliberately different from inflating NPC levels by elapsed calendar time. People become known to the player through introductions or sourced dispatches, not by printing the whole private list.
## High-fantasy sites and sacred traditions (v0.9 authoring)

The living world must visibly contain more than trade corridors and committee politics. `world/mythic.js` now locates fourteen distinct named sacred groves, sealed dungeons, rift labyrinths, spirit crossings, primordial forges, dragon sanctuaries, ancient beast courts and pilgrim ruins inside established atlas regions, with actual local ecology, danger and traditions. Six public religious customs and five named realm *teachings* have geography and disagreement; they do **not** settle the underlying secret cosmology or force a divine miracle. Mythic site ward/seal and Vael-charge states change on the same saved world ticks as other regional systems; public rumours arrive with delays.

Ordinary people have lives around these wonders. Trade routes near active ruins attract surveyors and pilgrims; dragons have protected nesting corridors; mortal petitioners cannot compel sovereign beasts or gods; shrine orders must budget for floods and ward repairs. A temple or academy may conceal a half-remembered evolutionary clue, but research must retrieve **precommitted truth** and the player/partner must undertake the risks to make it real.

Full cosmology (universe origin, divine limits, afterlife, plane boundaries and actual entry procedures) is deliberately **unresolved** pending source-consistent GM rules; public beliefs may conflict. This is clearly partial coverage of Master Prompt §3, not a claim that cosmological creation is finished. See `docs/FANTASY_CANON_AUDIT.md` for original §1–57 coverage.
