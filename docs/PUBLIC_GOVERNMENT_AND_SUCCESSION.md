# Beastbound public offices, actual mandates and lawful royal succession

**New open civic channel:** `social.petition({statement})` now accepts any genuinely expressed public concern at Morrowfen Crossing's newly authored public **March Council** correspondence counter, without requiring a guild, noble family or training fee. The public council acts independently on a real sixday; `social.petitionStatus` is read-only and `social.pickupPetition({petitionId})` requires in-person collection of its authentic decision. A public docket gives no automatic court win, law, rank, powers or standing. This is one actually playable form of political social mobility for ordinary residents, not a scripted noble invitation. See `docs/OPEN_CIVIC_PETITIONS.md`.


## What the original setting demands versus new authored names

The Unified Master Prompt §34 requires **multiple genuinely different governments**, including kingdoms, empires, republics, city-states, theocracies, confederations, sovereign intelligent-beast polities and independent territories. Each must have governing institutions, historical legitimacy, ruling dynasties where appropriate, succession practice, foreign relations, family organisations and living obligations. §§19, 24, 39, 42 and 54 prohibit conflating birth, power, official rank and player authority. The source does **not** supply a complete named current monarch and elected speaker for every one of the prototype's 24 states.

`world/atlas.js` previously authored 24 regional polities and distinct political systems. `world/social-houses.js` independently authored 28 households. **This patch authors a public civil office registry of 24 officeholders** linked to the *existing* states. When the atlas already names a true civic governing body (such as the March Council or Hearth-Court), the register reuses it; where the atlas only listed a leading academy, guild or research institution, the register explicitly authors a distinct **new** civil body rather than pretending that school is the government. The new rulers' names were not pre-existing ancient canon. `world/government-offices.js` holds the public office title, actual incumbent, governing institution, constitutional mode and limited local leadership roster. Its officeholders include council speakers, craft-republic chairs, guild moderators, independent-beast speakers, caretakers and crowned sovereigns. A family's charter name is not a completed succession and commoner representatives remain meaningful candidates. People may decline a role in future, but no such unverified action is manufactured by this limited simulation.

The social model distinguishes:

| Constitutional process | Existing example | What the engine actually does |
|---|---|---|
| **Hereditary sovereignty** | Asterwyn Crown, Red Dune Sultanate, Sunforge Principality | A reigning person is publicly recorded. No **automatic new heir, coronation, death, marriage, coup, house status or awakened bloodline** every 15 years. A long reign may prompt a public obligations review while the actual incumbent stays in office. Real succession needs later law-, genealogy-, court- and consent-backed events. |
| **Election / chartered mandate** | Mirror Gulf League, Pearl Bight Republic, Kareth forge republic | An existing public chair may retain a legal mandate or a new named candidate may be selected through its civic charter. A recorded change happens only when the named officeholder really changes. |
| **Consensus / council mandate** | Reedmarch Compact, Crownspine Holds, autonomous reef/Old Tracks polities | A convenor or mediator is a representative, **not automatically a hereditary sovereign**. Renewals and documented caretaker selections use the polity's actual public government style. |
| **Appointed mandate** | Frostmarch Protectorate, Ashglass military regency, Iron Citadel oligarchy | Leadership rotates or is replaced by an appropriate public appointment process, not a fiction that everyone has an open democratic election. |

`world/simulation.js` drives these processes using the **existing independent world RNG, monthly calendar, country legitimacy and stability, and house influence**. The player neither supplies a successor nor determines the leadership result. One known family member may be eligible as a *candidate in some institutions* but has no exclusive right to office; a named non-family challenger may be chosen and incumbents can retain mandates. The outcome isn't proportional to a character's cultivation rank. A legal transfer records previous and new officeholder, day, office and public publication date. **The `changesOfGovernment` counter increases only for a real change of office**, not automatically for every meeting.

## Correction of a specific earlier world-simulation bug

Previously the prototype incremented `rulerTenure` for all countries and after 180 months emitted a generic *"court succession"* even if nobody had died, resigned, been lawfully displaced or succeeded. It also counted every routine council renewal as a change of government. These were implausible, mechanically invented outcomes. Hereditary states now retain the actual reigning name indefinitely unless a separate verified, consent- and law-aware succession operation eventually exists. At 180-month review intervals, a public obligations review can occur **without** a new ruler. Sustained turmoil may trigger an open constitutional court inquiry but likewise does **not** materialise a new heir or strip someone of office by fiat. On recovery, the inquiry can close while the same ruler stays in office. Nonhereditary institutions still operate ordinary timed mandates, with the leader potentially remaining or changing; a change is tracked only if one truly occurs.

Existing `table.realm` saves without an individual public-office registry can be read **without changing any stored bytes**. When the world actually advances, a present-day public register is created and explicitly marked as *a newly reconstructed current listing*, not a forged historical lineage or proof of earlier coronations.

## What the player can know and do

`mode:"rule",system:"world",operation:"governmentStatus",params:{}` is a **strictly read-only local public office view**. It reports the actual visited region's government, named current officeholder, legal process, public mandate and genuinely released local announcements. `world.realmStatus` also includes the same local civic office. No secret genealogy, concealed royal illness, private war preparations, unpublished diplomatic clocks, internal government budget or supernatural inherited trait is revealed. A request that supplies a made-up king, remote secret office or chosen appointment is rejected without rolling or saving.

The player is **not required to join a family or enter politics**. The newly named officeholders are an institutional context for future free-form petitions, public hearings, trade, guild progression, justice, inter-family disagreement and diplomatic encounters. This patch does **not** yet claim to provide actual throne succession hearings, elections with player candidacy, detailed constitutional courts, legal marriage or hereditary disease modelling. It does not edit saved campaign histories, force relationships, or give a family free Qi, Mana, contracts or beasts.
